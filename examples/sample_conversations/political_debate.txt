Moderator: Senator Williams, you've called the proposed climate bill reckless. What specifically concerns you?
Senator Williams: What concerns me is the 800 billion dollar price tag and the projected loss of 200,000 jobs in the energy sector. We cannot mortgage our children's future or sacrifice working families on the altar of ideology.
Senator Chen: With respect, Senator, those job loss figures are from an industry-funded study. The independent CBO analysis projects a net gain of 400,000 clean energy jobs by 2030. You're citing numbers designed to frighten people.
Senator Williams: I'm citing numbers from people who actually work in those communities. People whose livelihoods you seem comfortable eliminating with a stroke of a pen.
Senator Chen: No one is comfortable with displacement. That's why the bill includes a 40 billion dollar transition fund specifically for affected workers. But I notice you never mention the cost of inaction — the 23 trillion the Federal Reserve estimates climate disruption will cost by 2050.
Senator Williams: The Federal Reserve is a bank, not a climate authority. And that 2050 projection is a model, not a fact. We shouldn't be destroying real jobs today based on speculative models.
Senator Chen: Climate science is not speculation. Ninety-seven percent of climate scientists —
Senator Williams: Don't cite consensus science at me as if that settles economic policy. We can agree on the science and still disagree on whether this particular bill, written by lobbyists for the solar industry, is the right response.
Senator Chen: Now that's interesting. You just acknowledged the science. So we agree the problem is real. Can we at least agree on that baseline?
Senator Williams: I've never said the climate isn't changing. My position is that the solution needs to be economically sustainable and nationally sovereign, not dictated by international agreements that don't hold China and India to the same standards.
Senator Chen: That's actually a reasonable point about international equity, and it's a conversation worth having. Would you support the bill if it included stronger reciprocal obligations for major emitters?
Senator Williams: I'd be willing to look at that. I'm not opposed to action — I'm opposed to bad policy.
Moderator: That exchange seems to have opened some common ground. We'll return to specifics after the break.
